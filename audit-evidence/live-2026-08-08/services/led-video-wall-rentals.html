Common Event Sizes & Pixel Pitch Guide

Choosing the right pixel pitch ensures your content looks crisp from every seat. For intimate spaces and close-viewing activations, 1.5mm–2.5mm delivers broadcast-quality resolution. Mid-range viewing (10–30ft) works best with 2.9mm–3.9mm. For outdoor festivals and large venues where viewers are 50ft+ away, 6mm–10mm provides excellent brightness and cost efficiency. Our team helps you match the right panel to your venue and audience.

Content Formatting Tips

Delivering content that looks stunning on LED requires the right formats and workflow. We recommend ProRes 422 or high-bitrate H.264 for video playback. Content should be rendered at the exact pixel dimensions of your wall configuration to avoid scaling artifacts. For live VJ performances, we integrate with Resolume, TouchDesigner, and proprietary media servers, ensuring frame-synced playback across multi-panel builds. Our team handles pixel mapping, color calibration, and content testing before your event.

Frequently Asked Questions

+How far in advance should I book?

We recommend booking at least 4–6 weeks in advance for standard rentals and 8–12 weeks for custom productions. Last-minute requests are often accommodated depending on availability.

+Do you travel outside Portland?

Absolutely. While we're headquartered in Portland, OR, we regularly serve clients across the West Coast and nationwide for touring and large-scale events.

+What's included in the rental?

Every rental includes delivery, setup, on-site technical support, and strike. Custom packages can also include content design, pre-visualization, and dedicated project management.

+Can you build custom shapes and configurations?

Yes. We specialize in non-standard LED builds including curved walls, columns, infinity mirrors, and architectural integration. Our design team creates pre-visualizations before fabrication.

+What content formats do you support?

We support ProRes, H.264, HAP, and Notch for video. For live VJ workflows, we integrate Resolume, TouchDesigner, and proprietary media servers with frame-synced output.
