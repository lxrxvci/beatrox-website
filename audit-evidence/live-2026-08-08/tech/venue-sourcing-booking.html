What is Venue Sourcing & Booking?

Venue sourcing is the search for a physical space that can actually hold your event — creatively and technically — a search Beatrox runs from Portland, Oregon for productions nationwide. It's easy to find a room; it's harder to find one with the footprint, ceiling height, power, load-in access, and neighborhood character your experience demands, at terms your budget can survive. Booking is the second half: negotiating the contract, deposits, insurance requirements, and house rules that govern what you're allowed to build. For experiential work, this step carries extra weight. Immersive environments, projection mapping, and interactive installations impose requirements a standard ballroom rarely meets — which is why the most memorable activations often happen in raw warehouses, shuttered retail, rooftops, and public parks. Those spaces deliver unmatched creative freedom, but they also demand a producer who can assess feasibility and negotiate with landlords and municipalities who don't deal with events every day. You need venue sourcing support whenever the space itself is part of the concept — or whenever you simply can't afford to discover a venue's deal-breakers after the deposit clears.

What BEATROX Provides

We start with the experience you want to create, then search for spaces that can support it — surveying candidates for power, rigging, footprint, load-in, sound restrictions, and neighborhood fit before anything goes on your shortlist. We negotiate terms, coordinate insurance and compliance requirements, and stay on as the liaison between you and the property through build, event, and restoration. Because we're builders, our feasibility reads are practical: we know what it costs to make a raw space show-ready, so there are no surprises in the budget. This approach shaped projects like MyShelter, where we converted a shut-down, narrow Manhattan bodega — previously used for storing old kitchen equipment — into a fully immersive forest environment for the Adidas Outdoor jacket launch, hiding all technical infrastructure within a tiny footprint. For AKU World, we took over a New York venue and programmed it into multiple distinct rooms — projection-mapped main room, interactive lobby, holographic merch space, live music, panels, and a patio bar. And Run for the Oceans placed a custom container installation in Domino Park, Brooklyn, coordinating a public-park footprint for Adidas and Parley Ocean School's annual 5k event.

Frequently Asked Questions

+Can you work with a venue we've already found?

Yes. We'll run a production feasibility survey on your space — power, rigging, load-in, restrictions — and negotiate or review the contract terms so you know exactly what the venue will and won't allow before you commit.

+Do you work outside of Portland?

We source and book venues nationwide. Our portfolio includes raw retail spaces in Manhattan, public parks in Brooklyn, studios in Miami and Los Angeles, and everything in between.

+What makes a venue right for an immersive or experiential event?

Beyond capacity: ceiling height and rigging options, available power, sound and neighbor constraints, load-in access, and how much the landlord will let you transform. We assess all of it during the survey, not after the deposit.

+How early should venue sourcing start?

As early as possible — the venue constrains everything downstream. For non-traditional or public spaces especially, we recommend starting the search 10–12 weeks before your target date.
