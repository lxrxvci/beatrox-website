What is Realtime Content (AR, VR, XR)?

Realtime content is digital media that renders live, frame by frame, in response to what's happening in the room — built by our Portland, Oregon team for experiences nationwide: a guest's movement, a performer's music, a sensor reading. Unlike pre-rendered video, nothing is fixed: the content is generated by an engine in the moment, so every interaction can be unique. AR (augmented reality) layers digital content over the real world, VR (virtual reality) builds fully digital worlds, and XR (extended reality) blends both with physical stages, screens, and projection. In live events, these technologies power AR mirrors, interactive projection worlds, virtual production stages, and immersive brand environments that guests can influence directly. You need real-time content whenever the experience should feel alive rather than played back. It's the difference between watching a brand film and stepping inside one.

Frequently Asked Questions

+What's the difference between AR, VR, and XR?

AR overlays digital content on the real world, VR is a fully digital environment, and XR covers everything that blends the two — including projection-mapped rooms and virtual production stages. We help you choose the right format for your goals and venue.

+Does real-time content require special hardware?

Yes — real-time systems run on dedicated media servers or high-performance workstations, plus sensors for interaction. We spec, supply, and integrate all of it.

+Can the content be updated during the event?

That's one of the biggest advantages of real-time systems. Branding, messaging, and even entire scenes can be swapped live without re-rendering video.

+How long does it take to build a real-time experience?

Simple AR moments can come together in 4–6 weeks; full interactive worlds typically need 8–12. We scope timelines in the first consultation based on your creative ambition.
