What are Site & Floor Plans?

Site and floor plans are the scaled drawings that define where everything goes — drawings our Portland, Oregon team produces for events and experiential builds nationwide: stages, screens, scenic, seating, bars, back-of-house, cable runs, power drops, and the paths guests and crew will take through the space. They're the single document every department — production, AV, scenic, catering, security, the venue itself — works from. You need proper site and floor plans whenever an event occupies real space, which is always. A venue's marketing PDF is rarely dimensioned, never shows column locations accurately, and says nothing about power or rigging points. Building your layout on accurate surveyed measurements is what separates a smooth load-in from a day of shuffling trucks and redrawing on a whiteboard. Good plans also make the invisible visible: guest flow bottlenecks, sightline obstructions, cable paths that cross emergency egress, a projector throw that clips a chandelier. Finding those problems on paper costs nothing. Finding them on site costs a day.

BEATROX produces complete, scaled site and floor plan packages: surveyed base plans, production layouts with every technical system placed to the inch, power distribution and rigging overlays, guest flow studies, and compliance layouts showing egress and fire lanes. Because our planners are also the people who load in the show, the plans reflect real truck dimensions, real cable paths, and real crew workflows. Frequently Asked Questions

+Can't I just use the venue's floor plan?

Venue plans are a starting point, but they're frequently out of scale, missing columns and rigging points, and silent on power. We survey and verify everything so your production layout is built on measurements you can trust.

+What do I get in a site and floor plan package?

A scaled base plan, the full production layout, technical overlays for power, rigging, and cable, plus compliance views showing egress and fire lanes — issued in formats every vendor can open.

+How early should floor planning start?

As soon as the venue is confirmed. Layout drives budget, equipment lists, and creative decisions — starting early means the design develops around the real space instead of being forced into it later.

+Do you update plans as the show changes?

Yes. Plans are living documents through pre-production. We track revisions and reissue to all departments so no one is ever building from an outdated drawing.
